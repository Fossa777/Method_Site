Duckweed — dark-field illumination

Panoramic view of the small green fronds of duckweed, one of the smallest flowering plants. Their rounded outlines, numerous cells, and paler tissue regions are visible. Duckweed floats freely on the water surface and can multiply rapidly by producing daughter plants.

Feather-like Green Algae from a Marine Aquarium

Microscopic panorama of green algal growth from a marine aquarium, tentatively identified as Bryopsis. Delicate branching filaments form structures resembling tiny fir trees or feathers. Numerous green lateral branchlets extend from a central axis. A distinctive feature of these algae is their siphonous organization, without the usual transverse cell partitions.

Fibrous granulomas in gudgeon

Squash preparation of gudgeon (Gobio gobio) tissue. Multiple rounded structures of varying sizes with concentric arrangements of connective tissue fibers, consistent with fibrous granulomas.

Filamentous Bacteria in a Fish Gill Smear

Microscopic panorama of a fish gill smear stained with May–Grünwald stain. Numerous thin filamentous and rod-shaped structures are visible, occasionally forming dense aggregates. Their morphology resembles bacteria associated with columnaris-like gill lesions. The microorganisms have not been identified to species level.

Fungal structures

A preparation of mucus and contents from the empty stomach of a black ghost knifefish (Apteronotus albifrons). Numerous filamentous fungal structures are visible among mucus, detritus, and small rounded elements. This finding indicates a marked presence of fungal forms in the gastric lumen and is pathologically significant. Captured with a 40× objective.

Granulomas in the Kidney of a Freshwater Angelfish

Microscopic panorama of kidney tissue from a freshwater angelfish (Pterophyllum scalare) showing granulomatous lesions. Areas of altered cellular material and darkly stained inclusions are visible among the renal structures. Acid-fast bacteria were detected during examination, suggesting a possible mycobacterial origin of the lesions. The bacterial species has not been identified.

Green Algal Growth in a Marine Aquarium

Algal growth collected from the outer surface of a water supply pipe in a marine aquarium. Under the microscope, long green filaments are visible among numerous golden-brown diatoms. These diatoms vary in shape and size, forming a diverse underwater community together with the filamentous algae.

Green Microalgae on Freshwater Aquarium Glass

Microscopic panorama of green algal growth collected from the glass of a freshwater aquarium. To the naked eye, these algae appear as small green spots firmly attached to the glass. Under the microscope, numerous rounded cells form dense clusters. Green chloroplasts responsible for photosynthesis are visible inside the cells.

Hyphal and sporangial structures

A high-magnification May–Grünwald-stained preparation of intestinal contents. Hyphal structures and rounded sporangial forms are visible among mucus and fragments of food material. Together with the massive presence of yeast-like cells in the gastric contents, this finding indicates marked fungal involvement of the digestive tract. Captured with a 100× objective.

Intracellular parasites 100x

Chrysiptera liver smear 100x. May–Grünwald stain. Parasites in histiocytes.

Labidochromis

Scales of the fish Labidochromis yellow.

Leaf-like Brown Algae from a Marine Aquarium

Small leaf-like growths collected from the wall of a tropical marine aquarium. Under the microscope, thin brown blades composed of numerous cells are visible. The cells form rows that spread out in fan-shaped patterns. Unlike filamentous algae, these algae form broad thalli resembling tiny leaves.

Liver of Apteronotus albifrons with granulomatous lesions

A squash preparation from the intestine of Apteronotus albifrons. Multiple granulomatous lesions associated with a mycobacterial infection are visible among mucus and sloughed mucosal material. However, the etiology of such lesions cannot be determined from morphology alone without additional investigations.

Liver with lipid inclusions

A squash preparation of the liver of a neon tetra (Paracheirodon innesi). Numerous rounded lipid inclusions, uneven tissue coloration, and areas of marked blood filling are visible. This pattern reflects severe hepatic alteration and should be interpreted together with other findings. Captured with a 10× objective.

Microworms

Microworms in the brain membrane of a ray, 20x. Small nematodes.

Modified carbol fuchsin staining without acid decolorization

Intensely stained red-pink rod-shaped bacteria are visible in the tissue material against a contrasting background. Acid decolorization was not performed, so acid-fastness was not assessed. Try to find them all...

Omul (Coregonus sp.) — gill anatomy

Panoramic preparation of the gill apparatus of an omul (Coregonus sp.). The gill arch, numerous gill filaments (primary lamellae), and secondary gill lamellae are visible. At higher magnification, the regularly arranged secondary lamellae can be clearly distinguished; they provide the principal surface for gas exchange.

Pigmented fungal structures on crab gills

Panorama fragment of crab gills showing branching septate hyphae and numerous pigmented rounded structures. Macroscopically, affected areas of the gills were black and showed tissue destruction.
