Arrowhead (Sagittaria sp.)

Panoramic view of part of an aquarium arrowhead leaf. Numerous plant cells containing green chloroplasts, longitudinal veins, and a toothed leaf margin are visible. Zooming in reveals individual cell shapes, cell walls, and the arrangement of chloroplasts.

Arrowhead (Sagittaria sp.) — 20×

Panoramic view of part of an aquarium arrowhead leaf. Numerous plant cells with distinct walls and green chloroplasts are clearly visible. The image shows individual cell shapes, chloroplasts arranged along the cell walls, and pale central regions occupied by large vacuoles.

Arrowhead (Sagittaria sp.) — 20×, polarized light

Panoramic view of part of an aquarium arrowhead leaf in polarized light. Numerous plant cells, longitudinal veins, and pointed teeth along the leaf margin are visible. Polarized illumination produces golden tones and emphasizes cell boundaries, offering a different view of the leaf structure.

Black brush algae (presumably Audouinella) — 10×

General view of the bush-like thallus of a red alga, provisionally identified as Audouinella sp. The attachment region with rhizoids is visible at the top of the image. Zooming in reveals branching cellular filaments, transverse septa, and putative monosporangia.

Black brush algae (presumably Audouinella) — 20×

A branching network of multicellular filaments. The branching pattern, cellular segmentation, and rounded reproductive structures on short lateral branches are clearly visible.

Black brush algae (presumably Audouinella) — 40×

Detailed structure of the branching filaments of a red alga. Individual cells, transverse septa, and rounded structures on lateral branches, presumably monosporangia, are clearly visible.

Branching filamentous bacteria — 100×

Spleen impression smear. Ziehl–Neelsen staining. 100× objective. Fuchsin-positive rod-shaped and filamentous structures showing prominent branching and uneven staining. The morphology is suggestive of branching actinomycetes, including Nocardia.

Branching rod-shaped bacteria — 40×

Spleen impression smear. Ziehl–Neelsen staining. 40× objective. Fuchsin-positive rod-shaped and elongated structures showing apparent branching. The morphology is compatible with Nocardia or other actinomycetes.

Capsalid — internal structures in relief

A second panorama of the same specimen with more pronounced relief. The outlines and spatial relationships of the internal organs are more clearly visible, especially the reproductive structures, intestinal ceca, and structures of the posterior body region.

Capsalid — whole mount

Panoramic whole-mount preparation of a capsalid monogenean. The pharynx, intestinal ceca, vitellaria, ovary, testes, and posterior attachment organ — the haptor — are clearly visible.

Chum salmon kidney — relatively preserved structure

Panoramic compression preparation of the kidney from a clinically healthy chum salmon after euthanasia. The renal tubules retain their usual structure and relatively clear lumina, while blood is distributed comparatively evenly throughout the tissue. The specimen serves as a control for comparison with pathological tubular changes.

Clownfish larva (Amphiprion sp.) — dark-field illumination

Panoramic view of a clownfish larva approximately 3 mm long. Its transparent body reveals internal structures, large eyes, the vertebral column, pigment cells, and developing fins.

Clownfish larva (Amphiprion sp.) — polarized light

Panoramic view of a clownfish larva approximately 3 mm long. Its transparent body reveals internal structures, large eyes, the vertebral column, pigment cells, and developing fins. Polarized light emphasizes tissue structure and produces distinctive colors.

Degenerated Gills of a Dead Fish

Even markedly postmortem-altered gills may retain diagnostically relevant findings. Attached monogeneans can still be detected, helping to reveal potential parasitic threats and indicating what should be looked for in living fish.

Diatoms from a Marine Aquarium

Microscopic panorama of a brown film collected from the glass of a marine aquarium. Numerous diatoms of different shapes, sizes, and structures are visible. Most have elongated or oval cells. Their golden-brown chloroplasts are enclosed within transparent silica shells, which display fine lines and intricate patterns.
